Connectome-inspired reservoir computing

FlyBrain Reservoir

In 2024, FlyWire and an international consortium published the first complete wiring diagram of an adult brain — every neuron and synapse in a male fruit fly, mapped by electron microscopy. Below is a working circuit built in that likeness: a recurrent network shaped like the fly's own neuropils, used as a living memory that can be taught, from only a handful of noisy samples, to reconstruct an entire function it has never fully seen. Sweep a signal across its "eyes," watch the activity ripple through mushroom body and central complex, and see how its guess stacks up against classic interpolation methods.

Explore: the edge of chaos

Reservoir computing has a signature result — recurrent networks work best balanced right at the boundary between order and chaos. Rescale this same brain's connections across a range of spectral radii and see where it peaks.

This scan fixes the leak rate high (fast, non-leaky updates) so the transition shows clearly. Too low a spectral radius and the network's memory fades before it can hold useful context; push it too high and activity turns chaotic and washes out the signal. The sweet spot in between — the "edge of chaos" — is where biological circuits are hypothesized to operate too. Your own leak-rate slider above shows the other side of this: a slow, heavily-damped brain stays stable even at high ρ, which is part of why the low-leak default fits so well.
